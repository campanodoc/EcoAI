EcoAI Framework

Research & Findings

Empirical validation of the EcoAI Index in two surveys of upper-secondary students in southern Italy (403 and 198 respondents), with a sequential quantitative design.

Reading mode

General public Researcher
1

Research Design

This research set out to answer a concrete question: do young people in Italian secondary schools have the competences to navigate a world shaped by both ecological crisis and artificial intelligence? To find out, we built a measurement tool, the EcoAI Index, and tested it in two surveys with 403 and 198 students.

1

Exploratory Phase — 403 students

Ariano Irpino, May–June 2025. We administered a first questionnaire to understand how students relate to sustainability and AI. We then grouped them into clusters based on their responses.

2

Confirmatory Phase — 198 students

A refined questionnaire was administered to a second sample. This phase tested the five-dimension structure of the EcoAI Index and its relationships, with preliminary results.

The study adopts a sequential quantitative design with two administrations serving distinct analytic functions: an exploratory phase for instrument development and cluster profiling, followed by a confirmatory phase for structural validation.

Survey Q1 — Exploratory N = 403

Students were grouped by how they approach sustainability and AI. Three distinct profiles emerged for each theme.

Method

K-means cluster analysis on Likert batteries. Two independent cluster solutions: sustainability orientation (3 groups) and AI stance (3 groups).

The Five EcoAI Index Dimensions

The EcoAI Index is a measurement tool that captures five distinct aspects of eco-digital competence, from how well students evaluate online information to whether they feel part of a broader human-technology-nature network. Each dimension is scored on a 1–5 scale.
Five latent constructs measured via Likert items (1–5), estimated through CFA with DWLS estimator. Reliability reported as McDonald's ω; convergent validity via AVE. Index weights are the normalised loadings of a one-factor model on the five construct means (sum = 1.00).
INFO

Eco-Digital Information Literacy

How well students search for, evaluate, and critically use information on environmental and civic issues through digital sources.

Competences in retrieval, critical evaluation, and purposive use of eco-digital information across digital platforms. Exogenous variable in the structural model. Lowest reliability of the five scales (ω = 0.662).

ω 0.662 AVE 0.399 Index weight 0.160
AATI

AI Awareness & Critical Attitudes

Understanding how algorithms work, recognizing AI bias, and developing a critical but informed stance toward AI systems.

Algorithmic awareness, bias perception, and ethical orientation toward AI governance. Correlations with VAL (r=0.859) and EPAS (r=0.825) are among the highest in the model, and HTMT with VAL is 0.842.

ω 0.791 AVE 0.405 Index weight 0.215
VAL

Eco-Civic Values

A personal commitment to sustainability, collective environmental responsibility, and civic justice — values that guide eco-digital behavior.

Value orientation toward sustainability, collective environmental responsibility, and civic justice. Seven of eight items mention technology and the prescriptive wording makes social desirability the strongest rival hypothesis. Low-class mean: 2.797.

ω 0.839 AVE 0.519 Index weight 0.215
AGY

Eco-Digital Civic Agency

The actual willingness and capacity to act as a responsible citizen in eco-digital contexts — feeling capable of making a difference.

Perceived self-efficacy and behavioral readiness for eco-digital civic action. In the structural model it is predicted by AATI and predicts EPAS together with VAL. One item has a very low loading (AGY_6, λ = 0.198).

ω 0.776 AVE 0.381 Index weight 0.181
EPAS

Eco-Posthuman Agency Scale

A sense of belonging to wider networks — recognizing oneself as part of an interconnected human-technology-nature system, not just an individual user.

Subjective disposition to recognize oneself as node in hybrid human-technological-ecological networks; relational integration openness. Terminal variable in the structural model (R² = 0.860). Three items; psychometric status preliminary. HTMT with AGY is 0.880, above the conservative 0.85 threshold.

ω 0.729 AVE 0.535 Index weight 0.230

Structural Model

The five dimensions are associated in a pattern compatible with a sequence: information practices relate to AI awareness and values, which relate to civic agency and to the sense of belonging to a shared environment. These are associations measured at the same moment and not causal effects.
Structural model (DWLS, N = 151, standardised estimates, all paths p<.001). Fit: CFI 0.978, TLI 0.975, RMSEA 0.064, SRMR 0.082. The two paths to EPAS are of equivalent size and the data do not support a hierarchy between them.
