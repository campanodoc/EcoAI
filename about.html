Gianfranco Rubino

Sociologist. Researcher in AI literacy and ecoliteracy, ASNOR trainer.

Profile · Training · Collaborate · Publications · Contact

Profile

Sociologist, with a PhD in Learning Sciences and Digital Technologies (National Doctoral Programme, XXXVIII cycle, LUISS Guido Carli and University of Foggia). His research studies how artificial intelligence enters educational settings as a technical, social and environmental actor, and how to teach students and adults to read its functioning, biases and ecological impacts together.

The work develops on three levels that support one another.

Theory

Ecodigital Posthumanism, a theory in development on the co-constitution of ecologies, computational infrastructures and subjectivity. Current status: preprint.

Framework

The EcoAI Framework, a competence model in four dimensions (Systems Understanding, Ecological Understanding of AI, Identity Formation, Tool-Based Learning), aligned with GreenComp.

Instrument

The EcoAI Index, a questionnaire of five scales and 25 items, with preliminary validation on two surveys in secondary schools of southern Italy (403 and 198 questionnaires). The samples are school-based and not nationally representative.

A parallel line of research, the Algorithmic Oracle, studies how people and institutions delegate judgement to algorithmic systems and treat their outputs as authoritative without verifying them.

He taught Sociology and Sociology of Education at the Istituto Superiore di Scienze Religiose Metropolitano San Michele Arcangelo (Facoltà Teologica Pugliese, Foggia) from 2018 to 2022. He holds a master’s degree in Sociology (Methodology and Social Research Techniques) from the University of Salerno.

Training

Training grows out of the research. Gianfranco Rubino is an ASNOR trainer: he trains trainers in AI literacy for companies, schools and individuals, and sits on the ASNOR technical-scientific committee as an AI literacy and ecoliteracy expert. The programmes bring the EcoAI Framework approach into the classroom.

Who it is for

Schools (teachers and students), companies (teams and training managers), individuals and professionals, and those who train other trainers. Formats, durations and delivery modes are agreed with the client.

What participants learn

The programme follows the four dimensions of the Framework. Systems Understanding develops the reading of ecological and digital systems and their feedback loops. Ecological Understanding of AI foregrounds energy consumption, infrastructures and environmental justice of AI systems. Identity Formation concerns bias, fairness and algorithmic responsibility. Tool-Based Learning develops situated and critical use of AI tools, including generative ones.

How we work

The European reference is GreenComp, integrated with AI literacy models. Programmes use four-level rubrics (Basic, Intermediate, Advanced, Autonomous-Critical) as teaching tools associated with the constructs, distinct from developmental stages or a linear progression. Where useful, the EcoAI Index records a group profile before and after the programme: it returns a collective picture, to be read as monitoring within the limits of a still preliminary validation.

Training for trainers

Programmes for those who teach AI literacy: conceptual framework, environmental and social impacts of AI, design of teaching units, criteria for assessing tools before adopting them.

Collaborate

The same approach is available for joint work with institutions, training bodies, companies and research groups.

Assessment of AI systems in educational settings

Analysis of AI tools adopted or to be adopted in schools and training bodies, on three levels: direct environmental impact (energy, hardware, resources), media effects (attention economy, infrastructural power) and pedagogical implications (datafication, algorithmic governance, surveillance). AI is treated as an actor to be problematised.

Measurement with the EcoAI Index

Administration of the Index to groups of students and reading of the collective profile across five dimensions (INFO, AATI, VAL, AGY, EPAS). The Index describes the declared orientations of a group and leaves individual competence assessment out.

Scientific consultancy and research projects

Participation in committees and working groups, co-design of programmes and studies, replication of the Index on independent and representative samples, integration of qualitative methods.

Status of validation

The validation of the Index is preliminary: the available samples are school-based and not nationally representative, the data are cross-sectional and self-reported, and replication on independent samples remains to be carried out. Research collaborations also serve to address this limit.
